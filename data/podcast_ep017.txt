ALEX: Before we get to our scenarios, let’s step back and learn something that’s been a silent driver of our portfolio’s performance – the daily reset decay in 3‑times leveraged ETFs.
SAM: Okay, but what does that actually mean in practice?
ALEX: Leveraged ETFs like our FANG+ 3×, S&P 500 3×, and Dow 3× are designed to deliver three times the daily return of their underlying index. That sounds powerful, but the math resets every night. If the index goes up 1 % one day, the ETF goes up roughly 3 %. The next day, the ETF starts from that new level and attempts to multiply the next day’s daily move by three again. Because of compounding, a series of volatile moves can erode value even if the underlying index ends up flat over a longer period.
SAM: So the decay is basically the loss we see when the market wobbles, even if the overall trend is neutral?
ALEX: Exactly. Imagine the index swings +2 % one day and –2 % the next. The daily leveraged returns would be roughly +6 % then –6 %, leaving the ETF about 0.36 % lower than it started, even though the index is back at zero net change. Over weeks of choppy trading, that small erosion compounds, and the longer you hold, the more pronounced the decay becomes.
SAM: How does that show up in our numbers?
ALEX: Our leveraged exposure sits at $152,730 CAD, which is 50 % of the portfolio. Using the anchor that a 1 % move in the underlying index translates to a ±$4,582 CAD swing in the leveraged positions, a modest 0.5 % daily volatility can shave off about $2,291 CAD per day if the market is range‑bound. Over a typical 20‑day month, that could amount to roughly $45,000 CAD of decay if the index doesn’t trend strongly in one direction. That’s why we monitor the VIX; sustained levels above 22 for two weeks trigger a scaling back of these positions to protect against decay.
SAM: So the decay is a hidden cost that we need to manage, not just a function of the market direction.
ALEX: Right. It’s a cost of leverage that’s built into the product design. Managing it means either reducing exposure when volatility is high or timing entries and exits around expected directional moves, like the upcoming ECB decision or the U.S. CPI release.
