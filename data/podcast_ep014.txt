ALEX:  Before we get to our scenarios, let’s step back and learn something that underpins all of this – **how the daily reset of 3× ETFs creates return decay in sideways markets**.

SAM:  I get that leveraged ETFs reset every day, but why does that matter if the market is just wobbling around?

ALEX:  A 3× ETF is designed to deliver **three times the daily return** of its benchmark. On day one, if the S&P 500 is up **+1 %**, the ETF gains **+3 %**. On day two, the ETF starts from that new base. If the market then goes **‑1 %**, the ETF loses **‑3 %** of the **new, higher** base, which is actually **‑3.09 %** in absolute terms. Over two days, the market is flat, but the ETF is down about **0.09 %**. That small loss compounds every day the index trades sideways or choppy, eroding value even though the underlying index shows no net change.

SAM:  So the decay is a math artifact, not a fee or expense?

ALEX:  Exactly. It’s a **geometric effect**. The more volatile the market, the larger the divergence between the leveraged ETF’s cumulative return and three times the index’s cumulative return. In a **high‑volatility, low‑trend environment**, you can lose **5 % to 10 %** of the ETF’s value over a month purely from the reset mechanism, even if the index ends the month flat.

SAM:  How does that tie back to our portfolio?

ALEX:  Our **leveraged exposure sits at $155,927 CAD**, about **52 % of the portfolio**. If we experience a **5 % month of sideways movement** with an average daily volatility of 1 %, the reset decay could shave **~$3,000 CAD** off that block alone. That’s why we monitor the **VIX** and consider scaling back into cash or CAD‑hedged ETFs when volatility spikes above **25** – it directly curtails the decay.

SAM:  Got it. So the lesson is: leveraged ETFs thrive in trending markets, but they’re victims of volatility. That’s why we need to be proactive with risk management.
